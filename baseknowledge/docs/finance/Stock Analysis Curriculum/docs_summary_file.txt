The Top Down Approach to investment analysis is a holistic method that starts with macroeconomic analysis and works its way down to specific stocks. The approach can be visualized as a pyramid with five floors:

1. Risk assessment (pyramid top)
2. Macroeconomic analysis
3. Sector identification
4. Fundamental evaluation of intrinsic value and corporate finance
5. Selection of the entry point and probability of winning

Using this approach allows investors to avoid getting caught up in individual stocks without understanding the broader market trends. It also helps investors know when to defend or attack their investments.

The model has two-dimensional support tools, including:

* Analysis floor: indicators such as macros, pain points, index support & resistance, VIX, and sector RRG charts
* Scan stock: tools such as barchart, IV_percentile, watchlist, Finviz, fundamental analysis (fair/under valued), and strike & probability (delta, support, valuation, and probability)

**Scan Strike & Probability**

* **Delta**: measures option's sensitivity to underlying asset price change
	+ Practical significance:
		- 0.70-0.80: Deep ITM, low leverage, ~70-80% win probability
		- 0.50 (ATM): Equilibrium point, 50-50 probability
		- 0.30-0.40: Slight OTM, balance between probability and profit
		- 0.15-0.25: OTM, ~15-25% win probability, higher leverage
		- <0.15: Far OTM, lottery ticket, low win rate
* **Golden Rule**: When selling options, choose a short strike with a Delta of 0.15-0.30 (~70-85% probability of running out of value). When buying options, choose a Delta ≥ 0.40 to avoid rapid option depreciation.
* **Probability of Profit (POP)**: measures the probability of a profitable position at maturity
	+ Criteria by strategy:
		- Short Put/Cash Secured Put: POP > 70%
		- Bull Put Spread: POP > 65%, Risk/Reward ≥ 1:1
		- Iron Condor: POP > 60% (with small profit), used when IV is high
		- Covered Call: POP > 75%, strike on nearest resistance
		- Long Call/Put: No POP, use Risk/Reward ≥ 2:1
* **Scan Strike theo Support & Valuation**:
	+ Identify technical support zone on the chart (weekly/daily)
	+ Place short put strike below support by at least 5-10% (the "no touch" zone)
	+ Check Delta of that strike, ideally between 0.20-0.30

**Vocabulary Table:**

1. **VIX**: CBOE Volatility Index, measures S&P 500's 30-day volatility.
2. **IV**: Implied Volatility, compares current level to 1-year history (in percentile).
3. **Delta**: Coefficient that measures option price change when stock rises/falls by $1.
4. **POP**: Probability of Profit, probability of a profitable position at expiration.
5. **DTE**: Days To Expiration, number of days until the option expires.
6. **Theta**: Time decay, value lost per day for an option.
7. **RRG**: Relative Rotation Graph, graph showing relative rotation between industries.
8. **Anonymous Historical Volatility**: Past actual volatility.
9. **IV Crush**: Sudden IV drop after a major event (e.g., earnings), causing losses for long options.
10. **Support/Resistance**: Important price zone where prices usually bounce off or are blocked.

**Options Strategies:**

1. **Short Put**: Selling puts with an obligation to buy shares if the price falls.
2. **Iron Condor**: 4-legs option strategy, making money when stocks are less volatile.
3. **Cash Secured Put**: Selling puts with enough cash to buy stocks, "buying" them cheaper.
4. **Covered Call**: Selling calls while holding stocks, increasing portfolio income.

This chapter focuses on translating analysis into trading decisions, particularly for options traders.

* **Delta**: A measure of option sensitivity. Key levels:
	+ 0.70-0.80: Deep ITM, high probability (70-80%) and low leverage.

	+ Covered Call: POP > 75% with strike on nearest resistance.
	+ Long Call/Put: No POP, use risk/reward ≥ 2:1.

1. Identify technical support zone on the chart (weekly or daily).
2. Place short put strike below support by at least 5-10%.
3. Check Delta of that strike; ideally, a Delta of 0.20-0.30.

**CHAPTER 6: SCAN STRIKE & PROBABILITY**

* This chapter translates analysis into specific trading decisions, crucial for option traders.
* **Delta**: measures option sensitivity to underlying stock price changes. Key ranges:
	+ 0.70-0.80 (Deep ITM): high win probability, low leverage
	+ 0.50 (ATM): equilibrium point, 50-50 probability
	+ 0.30-0.40 (Slight OTM): balance between probability and profit
	+ 0.15-0.25 (OTM): low win rate, high leverage
	+ < 0.15 (Far OTM): lottery ticket, very low win rate
* **Golden Rule**: when selling options, choose a short strike with Delta 0.15-0.30; when buying options, choose Delta ≥ 0.40.
* **Probability of Profit (POP)**: the probability of a profitable position at maturity. POP criteria for different strategies:
	+ Short Put/Cash Secured Put: POP > 70%
	+ Bull Put Spread: POP > 65%, Risk/Reward ≥ 1:1
	+ Iron Condor: POP > 60%, small profit
	+ Covered Call: POP > 75%, strike on nearest resistance
	+ Long Call/Put: No POP, use Risk/Reward ≥ 2:1

Overall, this chapter provides guidelines for selecting optimal strikes and evaluating the probability of profit in various trading strategies.

**Vocabulary Table & Quick Reference**

* Key terms:
	+ VIX: CBOE Volatility Index, measuring S&P 500's 30-day volatility
	+ IV: Implied Volatility, percentile comparison to 1-year history
	+ Delta: Coefficient measuring option price change when stock rises/falls by $1
	+ POP: Probability of Profit at expiration
	+ DTE: Days To Expiration, number of days until option expires
	+ Theta: Time decay, value lost each day due to option expiration
	+ RRG: Relative Rotation Graph, comparing industry rotations
* Concepts:
	+ IV Crush: sudden IV drop after major event (e.g. earnings), causing losses for long options
	+ Support/Resistance: important price zones where prices bounce or are blocked
	+ Options strategies:
		- Short Put: obligation to buy shares if price falls
		- Iron Condor: 4-legged strategy making money in low-volatility markets
		- Cash Secured Put: selling puts with enough cash to buy stocks, "buying cheaper"
		- Covered Call: selling calls while holding stocks, increasing portfolio income
